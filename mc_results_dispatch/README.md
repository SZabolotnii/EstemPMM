# pmm_dispatch() calibration (OPEN_ISSUES B-5, D-1), 2026-10-22

Generated by `dev_scripts/calibrate_dispatch.R 5000 9` (EstemPMM 0.5.0.9000 @ `79bc7a6`,
4.2 min on 9 cores) and `dev_scripts/analyze_dispatch.R`. Full printed output:
`analysis_output.txt`. `calibrate_dispatch.rds` (35 MB, 600 000 replications) holds every
replication and is not meant for git; rerun the script to regenerate it.

**Design.** y = 1 + 2x + e, x ~ N(0,1), 20 error laws (symmetric platykurtic, symmetric
meso/leptokurtic, skewed), n in {30, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000}, 5000 replications per cell.
Loss: squared error of the slope. Regret of a rule in a cell = MSE(rule) / min(MSE of OLS,
PMM2, PMM3). Rules are tuned on odd replications and scored on even ones.

## Findings

1. **The default rule loses in two places** (`cells_default.csv`).
   - Small-n platykurtic residuals: sample |gamma3| > 0.3 sends 26-36% of bimodal/uniform
     fits at n = 30 to PMM2 through the "asymmetric AND platykurtic -> PMM2" arm.
     Held-out regret is 2.67 (bimodal, n = 30).
   - Symmetric leptokurtic laws (logistic, Laplace, t5, t3): for n >= 100, PMM3 is the
     best method (MSE 0.62-0.94 of OLS). The default never picks it, because the PMM3 arm
     requires gamma4 < -0.7. Regret is 1.06-1.29.
2. **No retuned rule is uniformly better.** Fixed-threshold grid (1920 rules),
   n-scaled grid (|gamma3| > z*sqrt(6/n), 900 rules) and hybrids (384 rules) all trade
   the symmetric cells against skewed laws at n <= 50. Rules that are safe against OLS
   lose up to 25% there (exp n = 30), because at n = 30 a skewed law cannot be told
   reliably from a symmetric one.
3. **Two targeted amendments are a near-Pareto improvement** (`amendments_heldout.csv`,
   rule `M1M2_n100`). Every PMM2 arm of the default is kept, plus:
   - M1: symmetric residuals (|gamma3| <= 0.3) with estimated g3 < 0.95 and n >= 100 -> PMM3;
   - M2: "asymmetric AND platykurtic -> PMM2" only when |gamma3| > 2*sqrt(6/n), otherwise PMM3.

   Held-out mean log regret 0.061 -> 0.028, worst regret 2.67 -> 1.23. 28 of 120 cells
   are better by more than 3%; one is worse (exp, n = 30: regret 1.01 -> 1.04, still
   0.55 of OLS). Worst ratio to OLS is unchanged (1.12, near-Gaussian n = 30).
4. **D-1 (PMM2 beats OLS on symmetric heavy tails).** MSE ratio to OLS: t3 0.72-0.85,
   t5 0.92-0.98, Laplace 0.94-0.99, logistic 0.99-1.01. For Laplace and t5 the gain
   shrinks with n, so it is a finite-sample effect, not a breach of g2 = 1. For t3 the
   fourth moment does not exist and the g2 = 1 theorem does not apply. PMM3 does better
   still once n >= 100 (t3: 0.62-0.70).

## Several regressors (p_grid/)

Generated by `dev_scripts/calibrate_dispatch_p.R 2000 9` (EstemPMM 0.5.0.9000 @ `37e57e9`,
after the symmetric PMM3 solver gained its line search; 11.2 min on 9 cores) and
`dev_scripts/analyze_dispatch_p.R`; full output in `p_grid/analysis_output.txt`.

**Design.** Same 20 laws and n grid, p in {2, 6, 11} columns including the intercept:
y = 1 + 2 x1 + 0 x2 + ... + e, x ~ N(0,1), loss = squared error of the x1 slope; 360 cells,
2000 replications each (`calibrate_dispatch_p.rds`, not in git).

**Findings.**
1. **The calibrated rule carries over to p = 6 and p = 11.** Held-out mean log regret is
   0.029 (p = 2), 0.029 (p = 6) and 0.025 (p = 11), against 0.064, 0.053 and 0.043 for the
   0.5.0 rule. Worst held-out regret is 1.18 (0.5.0 rule: 2.46).
2. **No variant improves on it** (`p_grid/variants.csv`, 48 rules). Using the residual
   degrees of freedom n - p instead of n in both sample-size thresholds raises the held-out
   mean log regret from 0.0275 to 0.0292. Requiring n/p >= 10 before the leptokurtic PMM3 arm
   gives 0.0280; requiring n/p >= 20 gives 0.0296. Changing n_leptokurtic to 200, g3_threshold
   to 0.90 or clear_asymmetry_z to 1.5 is also worse. The defaults stay.
3. **The leptokurtic arm holds at p = 11.** At n >= 100 the rule is never worse than OLS by
   more than 0.7% on logistic, Laplace, t5 or t3. PMM3 alone has 0.81-0.89 of the OLS MSE on
   t3 and Laplace at n = 100, p = 11.
4. **Remaining losses are at n <= 50 near-Gaussian laws** (normal, triangular, Weibull(3.6)),
   where noisy cumulants send 42-66% of fits to PMM2 or PMM3. There the rule is up to 16%
   worse than OLS (1.18 on the held-out half), the same as the 0.5.0 rule (1.17).
5. **PMM3 falls back to OLS often at small n/p**: 12-38% of fits on logistic, Laplace, t5 and
   t3 at n = 30-50, p = 11. The fall-back is what keeps PMM3 within 13% of OLS there.

## Limits
- p = 2 for the original 5000-replication grid; p up to 11 with 2000 replications.
- Gaussian regressors, the slope of x1 only.
- The laws were chosen by hand.
- Time-series fits were not covered.
- Thresholds 0.95 / 100 / 2 were picked from a small grid, not optimised.
